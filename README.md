# Paper implementations

Three published M³ papers, each **implemented from scratch and validated against its own printed
numbers**. One directory per paper, all laid out the same way.

The point of the exercise is not to write code that runs. It is to answer, for every number a
paper prints, *does it come out again?* — and to say plainly which ones do not.

| | paper | method | gated targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Jung et al. (2026)](Jung2026_SpaceWeatheredMaria/) — *Remote Compositional Analyses of Space-weathered Lunar Maria*, PSJ 7(1) 18 | Hapke radiative transfer + CATMIP MCMC | **32 / 34** (+1 near miss) |
| 2 | [Fernández-Díaz et al. (2026)](FernandezDiaz2026_WaveletAE_FeO/) — *Global Lunar FeO Mapping via Wavelet–Autoencoder Feature Learning*, Mathematics 14(2) 254 | db4 wavelets + deep autoencoder + regression | **31 / 38** |
| 3 | [Korokhin et al. (2024)](Korokhin2024_ShkuratovANN/) — *Applying machine learning to a nonlinear spectral mixing model … M³ data*, PSS 244 105870 | Shkuratov mixing model + MLP trained on synthetic spectra | **46 / 65** (+6 near misses) |

All three are complete. A near miss is outside its tolerance but within 1.5 × it (or, for a stated
bound, meets it at the paper's printed precision); it is reported, not counted as a pass.

## Where to start reading

Read the documents in this order:

1. **Plain-language summary** — no assumed background. Start here. Papers 1 and 3:
   `README_Jung2026.md` and `README_Korokhin2024.md`, next to their folders; paper 2:
   `EXPLAIN.md` inside its folder.
2. **`README.md`** — the technical version: what each script does, how to run it.
3. **`RESULTS.md`** — the scorecard. **Generated by the audit script; never hand-edited.**
4. **`PAPER_NOTES.md`** — every deviation and finding, with the evidence for it.
5. **`LIMITATIONS.md`** (paper 1) / **`NEXT_STEPS.md`** (paper 2) / **`SOURCES_AND_LIMITATIONS.md`** (paper 3) — what was left
   out of scope and why, plus practical notes.

## Running them

```
python Jung2026_SpaceWeatheredMaria/scripts/run_all.py           # ~35 min
python FernandezDiaz2026_WaveletAE_FeO/scripts/run_all.py        # ~6 min once data is staged
python Korokhin2024_ShkuratovANN/scripts/run_all.py              # ~6-8 h (5-seed ANN)
```

Add `--fast` to either to skip the slow stages (MCMC; network + neural-net training).
Each ends in `audit_paper_targets.py`, which scores every reproduced value against the published
one and exits non-zero if a **gated** target misses its tolerance.

**There is no separate test suite — the audit is the test suite.** "Did it work?" is a command you
run, not an opinion. Every tolerance carries a written justification in `paper_targets.csv`, so a
target cannot be quietly loosened until it passes.

## What came out of it

### Paper 1 — the accuracy claims are largely met by the prior alone

The method returns a posterior distribution rather than a point estimate, which is what makes it
worth reproducing. But when the published accuracy claims are tested against a **prior-only
control** — abundances drawn from the Dirichlet prior, no data used at all — two of the three are
satisfied without looking at a single spectrum. A claim a random draw can meet is not evidence the
inversion works.

One gated target is a near miss and one fails:

- `S3_rms_all` (near miss) — a stated R² floor of 0.98; ours is **0.9797**, 0.0003 short. It
  meets the floor at the two decimals the paper prints.
- `S3_ilmenite_err` — a stated ≤10 percentage-point ilmenite bound; ours is **17.56**. This one is
  real. It fails under the converged sampler *and* under the authors' own released settings
  (12.89), so it is not an artefact of our correction, and fit quality is not the cause — R² runs
  0.983–0.994, at or above the published values.

Fifteen deviations are documented as D1–D15.

### Paper 2 — the arithmetic reproduces; the evidence for the design does not

The regression numbers come out, slightly *better* than published. What does not survive is the
justification for the two choices the paper is built on:

- **Table 1 is mathematically impossible.** It reports reconstruction error *rising* as wavelet
  coefficients are added back, ending at 0.0158 with all 83 kept. An orthonormal DWT that keeps
  every coefficient inverts exactly — measured **3.3 × 10⁻¹⁴** on 196,991 real M³ spectra. That
  rising trend is the only evidence offered for discarding the D1 coefficients.
- **The autoencoder makes the answer worse.** Feeding the 42 wavelet coefficients straight to the
  regressor gives MAE **1.595**; the full pipeline gives **1.986**. Two M³ bands and a fitted
  straight line give **1.685**. The compression stage — the paper's central contribution — loses
  information rather than distilling it.
- **The headline is the optimistic half of their own results.** The abstract's MAE 1.204 is a
  **five-sample** hold-out; their own 10-fold CV gives 2.29 ± 0.55. Both are in the paper.

All seven failing targets are PCA/ICA reconstruction values in the same direction — one coherent
finding, not scattered noise: our PCA is about twice as good as theirs while our autoencoder
matches theirs. PCA is deterministic, with no seed or training to blame.

Nine findings are documented as F1–F9. Three of them are **mistakes we made**, kept in the record
because the reasoning is worth more than the conclusion — including one where an independent
library caught a convention error in our own wavelet code that every self-test had passed.

### Paper 3 — the network maps what it was told to expect

The physics model, the paper's laboratory tests and the network's training behaviour all
reproduce, and the speed claim holds. The paper does not name its M³ image or give its test-site
coordinates; both were recovered from its own Figure 3 (M3G20090728T214215). What does not hold:

- **Three of the eight mapped quantities are set by the training ranges, not the spectra.** On
  synthetic spectra of known composition the network cannot recover orthopyroxene (R² 0.06),
  olivine (0.17) or the gray component (0.32); moving their training ranges moves the maps almost
  one for one. The published olivine and orthopyroxene maps sit at the midpoint of the range.
- **The "orthopyroxene" end-member is a pigeonite** by its own RELAB label; **Table 1 prints the
  olivine and pyroxene columns swapped** (RELAB's label for one mixture confirms it).
- **Where network and direct fit disagree, the direct fit is better supported**, and the
  non-uniqueness offered as the reason for the network disappears under the paper's own
  exhaustive search.

The authors' own M³ cleaning recipe (found reprinted in a co-author's PhD thesis), the LSCC
highland data and the four papers the comparisons depend on were obtained later and
implemented; they fixed the site-1 fit and two network numbers but none of the findings above
(F14). Findings F1–F14 and deviations D1–D22 are in its `PAPER_NOTES.md`.

## Conventions all three obey

Carried over from the rest of this repository, and load-bearing rather than stylistic:

- `data/` carries a stored SHA-256 manifest, re-verified at the **start and end** of every stage.
  Source folders are opened read-only; a change aborts the run before any output is touched.
- Band/wavelength correspondence is checked **positionally, never sorted**, and validated against a
  deliberately shuffled negative control.
- Rejected or unreliable values are written as NoData, never as numeric placeholders.
- `RESULTS.md` is written by the code that computed the numbers in it.
- Every stage takes an explicit seed; where a result is seed-dependent (neural-net training, random
  forests, MCMC), the spread is reported rather than one draw.

## Deliberately out of scope

- **Paper 2's global 806-orbit map** — multiple terabytes of M³ L2 strips. A data-transfer limit,
  not a method limit: every published *number* is checkable without it, and
  `step08_regional_map.py` maps a real 220 × 304-pixel block through the full pipeline and the
  two-band baseline side by side as the stand-in.
- Paper 3's full-scene Nelder–Mead map (~550 core-hours; the paper did not make one either).
- The other six papers on [`papers/M3-shortlist.txt`](papers/M3-shortlist.txt).

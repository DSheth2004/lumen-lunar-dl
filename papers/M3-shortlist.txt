M3 MINERAL PROPERTY IDENTIFICATION - SHORTLIST
Parth & Dhrumil | compiled 2026-08-31

Scope: Chandrayaan-1 M3 only. Journal implementation papers only (no conference,
no preprints). Publishers: IEEE / Elsevier / MDPI / Springer / Nature / Wiley-AGU.
Two categories, kept strictly separate:
  HAPKE = the Hapke radiative-transfer model does the retrieval.
  DL    = a trained neural network does the retrieval.

HAPKE  (5 of 5)
================================================================================

H1. Unmixing Mineral Abundance and Mg# With Radiative Transfer Theory:
    Modeling and Applications
    Sun & Lucey | J. Geophysical Research: Planets 126, 2021 | AGU / Wiley
    https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006691
    MINERAL PROPERTY : olivine, LCP, HCP, plagioclase vol% abundance + Mg#
    METHOD           : new optical constants for olivine/OPX/CPX; radiative
                       transfer with soil porosity and opposition effect; forward
                       + inverse modelling against a spectral library
    ACCURACY         : better than 3 vol% (olivine, LCP, HCP), 6 vol%
                       (plagioclase), 10 for Mg#
    WHY              : the reference implementation for quantitative M3 unmixing.
                       Publishes its own optical constants, states its error
                       budget per mineral, and delivers a global Mg# map from M3
                       images. Start here - everything else is a variation on it.

H2. Remote Compositional Analyses of Space-weathered Lunar Maria
    Jung, Lapotre, Milliken & Minson | Planetary Science Journal 7(1) 18, 2026
    | AAS, published by IOP  [note: outside your stated publisher list]
    https://doi.org/10.3847/PSJ/ae2b57
    MINERAL PROPERTY : modal mineral abundance WITH per-pixel uncertainty, space
                       weathering separated from composition
    METHOD           : probabilistic MCMC inversion of the Hapke model; weathered
                       surface = mixture of fresh + fully weathered components,
                       agglutinates as an explicit end-member; calibrated on
                       Apollo mare soils of known mineralogy, then applied to M3
    WHY              : the only paper here that returns a posterior instead of a
                       point estimate. Your Stage 06 gate rejects Olivine on a
                       best-minus-second-best margin, which has no statistical
                       meaning; this replaces it with a credible interval.

H3. Quantitative Mineral Maps of the Lunar South Polar Region Derived From the
    Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M3) Data
    Du, Liu, Tong, Jin, Xie, Feng, Jin & Zhang
    | J. Geophysical Research: Planets 131(1) e2024JE008842, 2026 | AGU / Wiley
    https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JE008842
    MINERAL PROPERTY : high-Ca pyroxene, low-Ca pyroxene, olivine, plagioclase
                       abundance at ~193 m/px
    METHOD           : radiative-transfer unmixing of a co-registered M3 + LOLA
                       cube built to survive extreme illumination geometry
    WHY              : newest end-to-end M3 abundance product for exactly your
                       four minerals, and the best worked example of retrieving
                       under bad illumination - where your 19 remaining shadowed
                       pyroxene pixels sit. Cross-checked against Kaguya SP.

H4. An Integrated Framework for Global Detection and Quantification of Lunar
    Pyroclastic Deposits
    Misra, Bhatt, Hess & Wohler | Planetary Science Journal 7(1) 24, 2026
    | AAS, published by IOP  [note: outside your stated publisher list]
    https://doi.org/10.3847/PSJ/ae317c
    MINERAL PROPERTY : volcanic glass abundance (~75 wt% at Aristarchus)
                       separated from the common mineral end-members
    METHOD           : reflectance -> single-scattering albedo via Hapke (2002);
                       submicroscopic iron (npFe0 + mpFe0) folded into the
                       end-member simulation; linear unmixing in the SSA domain
    WHY              : glass overlaps the silicates in VNIR, which is why a
                       melt-glass-rich scene contaminates a mineral call. This is
                       the published recipe for quantifying it instead of letting
                       it leak into your olivine and pyroxene fractions.

H5. A Novel Clustering-Based Methodology for Mapping Lunar Surface Minerals
    Using Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M3) Hyperspectral Data
    Messinios, Koutroumbas & Sykioti | Remote Sensing 18(5) 776, 2026 | MDPI
    https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18050776
    MINERAL PROPERTY : mineral identification + relative abundance - augite 55%,
                       impact melt-glass 31.4%, olivine 9.4%, plagioclase 4.4%,
                       ilmenite 3.4%
    METHOD           : Hapke photometric conversion to SSA, continuum removal,
                       per-pixel absorption-position detection, scene-wide
                       histogram of absorption locations, k-means on 6-D binary
                       absorption-pattern vectors
    WHY              : closest to the pipeline you already run - same 85 bands,
                       same 140 m/px, same band-parameter logic, with the SSA
                       conversion added in front. The absorption histogram is a
                       defensible way to decide which features a scene actually
                       supports, instead of assuming Band I and Band II are both
                       usable (yours: 26% both / 25% one / 49% neither).

DL  (4 verified - see the note at the bottom on the fifth slot)

D1. A Hapke Physics-Guided Deep Autoencoder for Lunar Hyperspectral Unmixing
    Lin, Liu, Han, Liu, Bo & Zhang | Remote Sensing 18(8) 1123, 2026 | MDPI
    https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18081123
    MINERAL PROPERTY : plagioclase, high-Ca pyroxene, low-Ca pyroxene, olivine -
                       fractional abundance per pixel
    METHOD           : PGU-Net. 1-D convolutional encoder with dual attention;
                       decoder does linear mixing in the SSA domain plus a
                       learned non-linear correction; abundances read off the
                       latent layer under sum-to-one and non-negativity
    IMPLEMENTABLE    : yes - blind unmixing, no labelled pixels needed. Inputs
                       are M3 L2 reflectance + an endmember library you have.
                       Runs on the Copernicus cube as-is.
    WHY              : the only DL paper that puts the physics inside the network
                       rather than beside it, so every abundance it emits is
                       physically admissible by construction. Its validation
                       ladder (synthetic -> Cuprite benchmark -> M3 vs Kaguya MI)
                       mirrors your Stage 01-06 structure.

D2. Applying machine learning to a nonlinear spectral mixing model for mapping
    lunar soils composition using CHANDRAYAAN-1 M3 data
    Korokhin, Surkov, Mall, Kaydash, Velichko, Velikodsky & Shalygina
    | Planetary and Space Science 244, 105870, 2024 | Elsevier
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2024.105870
    MINERAL PROPERTY : clinopyroxene, orthopyroxene, olivine, plagioclase
                       abundance + AVERAGE GRAIN SIZE + nanophase iron +
                       optical maturity, all per pixel
    METHOD           : Shkuratov (1999) non-linear mixing model as forward
                       simulator -> generate a large synthetic spectral set with
                       randomised mixture parameters -> train an MLP on it ->
                       apply the trained net as the inverse to M3. A flat grey
                       end-member absorbs agglutinate glass and photometric
                       residuals.
    IMPLEMENTABLE    : yes, and no ground-truth bottleneck - the training set is
                       synthetic, you generate it. Test area (Shapley) spans mare
                       and highland, like Copernicus.
    WHY              : the only paper in either list that SOLVES for grain size
                       instead of fixing it. That is the direct answer to your
                       Stage 01-02 result that particle size alone moves Band I
                       depth 5.1x, in opposite directions for mare and highland.
    NOTE             : uses the Shkuratov model, NOT Hapke. Do not cite it as a
                       Hapke implementation.

D3. High resolution olivine abundance mapping in the Copernicus crater combining
    spectral data of Chandrayaan-1 M3 and Kaguya MI
    Surkov, Mall & Korokhin | Planetary and Space Science 256, 106039, 2025
    | Elsevier | OPEN ACCESS (CC)
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2025.106039
    MINERAL PROPERTY : olivine abundance in vol% - 0 to 35 vol% across the
                       region, up to 90 vol% at central-peak boulder outcrops
    METHOD           : multilayer perceptron learns the M3 -> Kaguya MI
                       spectral-spatial correlation and super-resolves the
                       olivine map. One data frame per instrument, no mosaic.
    IMPLEMENTABLE    : yes, with one dependency - a co-registered Kaguya MI frame
                       over Copernicus.
    WHY              : your crater, your problem mineral, your instrument. Gives
                       you an independent published olivine abundance for
                       Copernicus to test your leave-one-reference-out gate
                       against, which is exactly what an external check needs.

D4. Global Lunar FeO Mapping via Wavelet-Autoencoder Feature Learning from M3
    Hyperspectral Data
    Fernandez-Diaz, Sanchez Lasheras, Gracia Rodriguez, Iglesias Alvarez,
    Marques Sierra & de Cos Juez | Mathematics 14(2) 254, 2026 | MDPI
    https://doi.org/10.3390/math14020254
    MINERAL PROPERTY : FeO abundance in wt% (global map at ~150 m/px)
    METHOD           : Daubechies-4 wavelet compression of each spectrum to 42
                       coefficients -> symmetric deep autoencoder to a 6-D latent
                       code -> supervised regression on the latent codes
    ACCURACY         : MAE ~1.204 wt% FeO; 10-fold CV plus a held-out 10% split
    IMPLEMENTABLE    : yes, but supervised - needs the 50 returned-sample FeO
                       values to train the regressor.
    WHY              : the wavelet-then-autoencoder front end is a principled way
                       to compress 85 M3 bands without hand-picking band
                       parameters. Also the only M3 DL paper with proper held-out
                       + k-fold validation, which is the standard your Stage 06
                       generalisation claim has to meet.


================================================================================
ON THE FIFTH DL SLOT
================================================================================

Four is what the literature actually supports, not where the search stopped.
Lunar deep learning is built on Kaguya MI, Chang'E-1 IIM, Clementine and Diviner,
because those come with dense returned-sample ground truth at Apollo, Luna and
Chang'E sites - which supervised training needs. Your own taxonomy shows it:
rows 1, 2, 7, 29, 40, 66 and 76 are all non-M3.

M3's advantage is spectral resolution, and that is exploited mainly by physics-
based inversion, which needs no labels. So the M3 DL papers that do exist are
nearly all physics-guided - D1 and D2 both wrap a scattering model into the
network.

Two candidates for a fifth slot, and the exact reason each is not in the list:

  - Ni, Zhao, Meng, Du, Lu & Wang, "Signal Compensation of M3 Under Low-
    Illumination Conditions Using a CycleGAN-Based Network", IEEE JSTARS 18,
    8504-8522, 2025.  https://doi.org/10.1109/JSTARS.2025.3549768
    Deep learning, M3, IEEE, open access - but it restores shadowed spectra and
    identifies NO mineral. No L4 property node. Promote it only if the brief is
    "DL on M3" rather than "DL for mineral property".

  - Fazel Hesar et al., "Connecting Meteorite Spectra to Lunar Surface
    Composition Using Hyperspectral Imaging and Machine Learning", Universe
    12(4) 93, 2026.  https://doi.org/10.3390/universe12040093
    Does identify olivine and pyroxene at 93.7% accuracy - but the learners are
    SVM and k-means, not deep, and M3 is used only for validation, not as the
    primary data.


================================================================================
EXCLUDED, AND WHY
================================================================================

Zheng et al. 2026, IEEE JSTARS - physics-guided diffusion unmixing. Chang'E-1
  IIM, not M3.
Han et al. 2026, Geo-spatial Information Science - two-layer Bayesian Hapke that
  inverts grain-size DISTRIBUTION as a continuous variable. Excellent method,
  but applied to Chang'E-3/5 in-situ spectra, not M3.
Ahmad et al. 2022, Planet. Space Sci. 220 - Chandrayaan-2 IIRS + M3 unmixing.
  Strictly LINEAR (N-FINDR + FCLS), no radiative transfer, so not a Hapke paper.
Patel et al. 2026, Icarus 451 - Chandrayaan-2 IIRS, classical linear unmixing,
  and carries a published corrigendum (Icarus 453, 117047).
Arun et al. 2023 (graph neural network, IIRS) and Majumdar, Purohit & Mall 2023
  (global pyroxene ML) - conference papers, not journal.
M3 stripe removal (IEEE TGRS 2022) and spatial-spectral 3D compensation
  (Remote Sensing 18(5) 682, 2026) - restoration, no mineral outcome.
